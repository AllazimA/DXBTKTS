01 · The short answer

Three ways DXB TKTS earns, one rule for clients

1

Margin inside published prices

Every price on an experience page already includes our earnings. The client pays one all-inclusive price — no booking fee added at checkout.

2

Concierge fee on arranged requests

When we arrange something that is not a catalogue product — a restaurant, an event, an itinerary, a sourcing request — we charge a coordination fee, shown in the proposal before the client confirms.

3

Commissions from suppliers

Many suppliers pay agents a commission. It is our income from the supplier side; it is never added to the client’s price and never changes what we recommend.

The client rule: the client always sees the full price before paying. Published prices are final (VAT included once we are registered). Arranged requests show every line and our fee in writing. Membership fees (Oasis, Pearl, Najm) are a fourth, separate income line and are not covered here.

02 · Published experience prices

What is inside a price on the website

We buy each experience from a licensed operator in one of two ways. The model is agreed per supplier and decides how the published price is built.

Model A — Net rate (we set the selling price)

The supplier gives us a confidential net rate. We add our margin and publish the result. Best for private and premium products (yachts, private safaris, chauffeurs, Signature experiences), where we add value and control the price.

Published price = Supplier net rate + DXB TKTS margin (+ 5% VAT once VAT-registered) DXB TKTS gross margin = Published price (excl. VAT) − Supplier net rate DXB TKTS contribution = Gross margin − card-payment fees − other direct costs

Model B — Commission (the supplier sets the price)

The supplier keeps its public price and pays us a percentage of each sale. Best for standard products with a public price (attraction tickets, some tours).

Published price = Supplier’s public price (rate parity — we do not undercut it) DXB TKTS commission = Published price × commission % DXB TKTS contribution = Commission − card-payment fees (when we collect the payment)
Benchmark (verified): GetYourGuide publishes a 20–30% commission range for its suppliers in Dubai. Suppliers already accept giving up this share to marketplaces — so asking for a net rate 15–25% below their public price is a normal, realistic negotiation for DXB TKTS.

Card-payment cost (verified)

Stripe’s published UAE pricing (November 2026): 2.9% + AED 1 per successful card payment, +1% for international cards, +1% if currency conversion applies. Most DXB TKTS visitors will pay with international cards, so this guide uses 3.9% + AED 1. The final provider (Stripe, Network International, Ziina, PayPal) is not chosen yet.

04 · Arranged requests (concierge)

Why we charge a fee — and how

An arranged request uses our time and relationships: calls, negotiation, confirmations, changes and being on call on the day. Many suppliers involved — restaurants, boutiques, venues — pay no commission at all. Without a fee, the most demanding requests would earn nothing. The fee is always shown in the proposal before the client confirms.

Our commission rules

  • Never added to the client’s price. A commission comes out of the supplier’s side.
  • Never changes the recommendation. We recommend the right option, even when it pays us nothing.
  • Disclosed in principle. The Services page tells clients that some partners pay us a commission. Amounts are commercially confidential.
  • Rate parity. For commission products we do not sell below the supplier’s public price; we compete on service, bundling and access.
  • In writing. Every commission and net rate sits in a signed rate sheet with validity dates.

06 · Protecting the margin

Rules that keep DXB TKTS profitable

  • Margin floor: do not publish a product whose contribution after card fees is below AED 150 or 12%, whichever is higher (tickets excepted as add-ons).
  • Sell private and Signature first: they carry 15–25% margin; shared products and tickets carry 4–16%.
  • Price extras generously: decoration, photographers, transfers and catering lift contribution by 30–50% on a booking.
  • Steer large payments to bank transfer: above roughly AED 10,000, card fees become significant.
  • Review prices each season: November–April is peak; update net rates and published prices together.

07 · VAT and compliance

What to confirm with advisers

  • VAT is 5% in the UAE. Registration becomes mandatory above AED 375,000 of taxable supplies in 12 months (voluntary from AED 187,500).
  • Principal or agent? When we resell on a net rate (Model A) we likely act as principal, so VAT applies to the full price. When we earn a commission (Model B) we may act as agent, so VAT applies to our commission. This changes margins (see Example 1) — confirm with a UAE tax adviser before launch.
  • Published prices must include VAT once registered, so the client’s price never changes at checkout.
  • Tourism licensing: confirm with Dubai’s Department of Economy and Tourism which licence DXB TKTS needs to sell experiences online.
